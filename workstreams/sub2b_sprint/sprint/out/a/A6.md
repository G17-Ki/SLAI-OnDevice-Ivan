**Stack M is the only stack with a clean licence sheet end to end.** Apache-2.0 for the
ASR, backbone, TTS and neural audio tokenizer; MIT for the VAD. No copyleft, no
non-commercial corpus, no research-only derivation chain, no mutable terms, and — because
MOSS needs no external G2P — no eSpeak GPL-3.0 linkage either.

This is an engineering licence review, not legal advice. Where a question is legal rather
than factual I record the facts and the question and do not answer it.

## R-1 — Every Piper voice descends from a research-only corpus 🔴

The most consequential finding in A6, and worse than the plan flagged.

Piper's **own** `MODEL_CARD` files establish the chain:

| voice | training |
|---|---|
| `en_US-lessac-medium` | **"Trained from scratch"** on the Blizzard 2013 Lessac corpus |
| `en_US-kusal-medium` | "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)" |
| `es_ES-davefx-medium` | "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)" |
| `fr_FR-siwis-medium` | "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)" |
| `ru_RU-dmitri-medium` | "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)" |
| `zh_CN-huayan-medium` | "Finetuned from U.S. English lessac voice (medium quality)" |

I fetched the corpus licence and archived it. It is a **Research Licence Agreement**:

> Licensors hereby grant to the User a non-exclusive and non-transferable licence,
> **without the right to sub-licence**, to use the Materials **exclusively for Research
> Purposes only** throughout the world.

"Research Purposes" is defined as "only those purposes associated with research and
exploration". Access requires per-organisation manual registration and approval.

The ES, FR and RU datasets being CC0 and CC-BY-4.0 does not help — those cover the
*finetuning* data. Every voice's *starting weights* come from lessac. Piper's repo-level
MIT label describes the packaging, not the derivation chain.

**Parameters behind this question: H 78,253,010 · Q 62,602,408 · F 62,602,408** — each
stack's entire TTS bank for the non-ZH languages.

**The legal question I cannot answer:** are TTS weights finetuned from a voice trained on
the Materials themselves a derivative of the Materials? That is genuinely unsettled and
this lane does not pretend otherwise. What *is* settled and archived: the corpus licence
is research-only and non-sublicensable, and the vendor's own metadata says every voice
descends from it. If the answer is yes, H, Q and F all lose their TTS bank.

Captures: `blizzard2013-lessac-research-licence_2026-08-02.txt` plus three MODEL_CARDs.

## R-2 — Matcha / Baker: non-commercial, from the model's own README 🔴

`csukuangfj/matcha-icefall-zh-baker` declares **no licence at all**, and its README says,
verbatim:

> **Note**: The dataset is for non-commercial use only.

Not an inference — the vendor's own statement in the model repo. Matcha is the ZH TTS
acoustic model in **D, Q and F** (18,665,803 each).

For Q and F an in-stack alternative exists — serve zh from the Piper `huayan` voice — but
that voice carries R-1 and R-4. There is no clean ZH TTS route in any of the three stacks.

## R-3 — Falcon-H1's terms are not established 🔴 (Stack F only)

Three separate problems, each sufficient on its own.

**1. Identity gap.** The repo carries **no LICENSE file**. Its `cardData.license_link`
points at `https://falconllm.tii.ae/falcon-terms-and-conditions.html`. I captured that
page (200, 158,108 B, sha256 recorded) and searched it exhaustively:

```
"Falcon H1"  : 0 occurrences
"Falcon-H1"  : 0 occurrences
```

The page carries licences for Falcon 2 11B, Falcon 180B, Falcon Mamba 7B and Falcon 3.
**The terms governing this model are not established.**

**2. Unilateral mutability.** The Falcon 3 licence — the closest candidate — defines:

> "Acceptable Use Policy" means the latest version **from time to time** of the policy
> designated as such hosted at FalconLLM.

and instructs:

> You **should monitor the web address** at which the Acceptable Use Policy is hosted to
> ensure that your use of the Work or any Derivative Work complies with the **updated**
> Acceptable Use Policy.

Output is bound too: *"You may not use the Work or any Derivative Work or any output from
the Work or Derivative Work, whether directly or indirectly, to create other works for
any purpose which conflicts with the Acceptable Use Policy."*

**3. Undischargeable for this product.** The target is an **offline, on-device** product.
A device already in a user's hand cannot be re-licensed when the AUP changes, and there is
no update channel that would make it so. The monitoring obligation cannot be met by
construction — not "risky", but structurally impossible for this product shape.

Worth stating plainly: **Stack Q's backbone has none of these problems and costs
11,149,184 fewer parameters.** Apache-2.0, LICENSE file in the repo, immutable,
model-specific. On licence grounds alone Q dominates F.

Captures: `falcon-terms-and-conditions_2026-08-02.txt`, `falcon3-tii-falcon-license_2026-08-02.txt`.

## R-4 — Piper zh huayan: corpus licence "Unknown" 🔴 (Stack H only)

Piper's own MODEL_CARD for `zh_CN-huayan-medium` records `License: Unknown` for its
dataset. An unknown training-data licence cannot be cleared, only avoided. H ships a
five-voice bank including huayan; Q and F ship four voices and serve zh from Matcha.

## Training-data provenance

Training-data licence is **not disclosed** for essentially every model here. That is the
industry norm; I record it uniformly rather than holding it against individual components.
The exceptions — where provenance *is* disclosed and *is* a problem — are exactly the RED
rows: Lessac (research-only), Baker (non-commercial), HuaYan (unknown).

## Capture policy

Every archived file carries `captured_utc`, `source_url`, `http_status`, **sha256 of the
raw bytes**, byte count, and the reason it was captured. A URL is not a record — which is
precisely why Falcon's terms needed archiving, and why archiving them is what revealed
that they never mention Falcon-H1.
